**What was delivered** (in `evals/claude_Fable_5_office_prompt_v2/`):

- `index.html` — the exact shell from the spec, scripts in the mandated order.

Every floor has a hallway-ring navigation graph with BFS pathing, a `sitTargets` table recording sit-vs-stand and the facing angle for every seat (desk chairs face the monitor at `rotation.y = π`, so legs go under the desk and backs rest on the backrest), plus chunky call panels and shaft indicators with cached canvas-texture digits.
- `elevator_logic.js` — the pure SCAN scheduler (no Three.js/DOM): five-state machine, direction-aware call handling, capacity-4 spot reservations, door hold/min/max timing, anti-starvation guards so a full car always leaves the lobby.
- `elevator.js` — visual adapter only: yellow translucent car, sliding doors, lit destination buttons, in-car indicator; every decision is delegated to the logic class.
- `sim.js` — simulated clock (lockstep time scale, default 120×), 9-keyframe day/night cycle with compressed golden hours and readable nights, 20 workers + 80 recyclable visitors with schedules, plan compilers, the deficit-driven visitor top-up, soft-repulsion collisions with boarding exemptions, seat reservations with explicit `RELEASE_SEAT`, stall-recovery in walks and boarding, the HUD with log-spaced speed and occupancy sliders, and a midnight reset that re-rolls agents *and* clears the elevator.
- `elevator_logic_test.js` — 7 deterministic Node tests covering all the required scenarios: **7/7 pass**.

**Verification beyond the required tests.** I built two headless harnesses (kept in `/tmp`, not shipped) that stub `THREE` and the DOM: one validates the world graph (every waypoint on every floor reachable, all sit targets well-formed, 20 desks), and one drives the *entire* browser stack through two simulated days. That second harness caught a real bug the unit tests couldn't: at 120×+ speed, a frame's `motionDt` exceeded the door-open window, so the elevator could open *and* close its doors inside one tick before any rider observed `DOOR_OPEN` — stranding a phantom passenger overnight. Fixed by making the logic's substep loop yield once when doors reach `DOOR_OPEN`, and letting `ENTER_ELEVATOR` complete all its phases within one frame. After the fix, full-day behavioral runs pass at 30×, 120×, and 600×: all 20 workers settle in by 10:00, 17–19 have lunched by 14:45, the visitor top-up sustains ~40 of the 45-person target, the building is completely empty by 21:00 with zero phantom passengers, and day 2 starts cleanly after the midnight wrap.

One deliberate deviation: the spec's `SIT` drop of ~0.35 assumed its own body proportions; with this person model (hips at 0.62, seats at ~0.46) the correct drop is 0.16 — the stated goal, hips aligned to the seat with legs forward under the desk, is what the code achieves.
